# 01 · Scientific brief

## The decision

The user is a chemist with a limited isolation budget. Their objective is to prioritize one plant-derived constituent for confirmatory work. The disease-relevant endpoint is inhibition of intracellular *T. cruzi* amastigotes. Host-cell cytotoxicity is a separate measurement. A parasite phenotype alone does not identify a protein mechanism. The original study discusses mitochondrial complex I as a hypothesis, with unresolved life-stage biology. [Isolation study](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4RA08652J)

## Why these data are unusual

An existing collection couples LC–MS/MS profiles from 1,600 plant extracts with anti-trypanosomatid screening. The original researchers identified rotenone and deguelin as active. A follow-up prepared fresh extracts, characterized and tested 41 isolated compounds, and measured cytotoxicity against rat L6 cells. Its table provides a rare check on the decision a chemist might have made earlier. These two studies are related by design; neither the plants nor the compounds are a random independent sample. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.3c00800) · [Follow-up, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4RA08652J)

| Compound | Parasite IC₅₀, µM | L6 IC₅₀, µM | Reported selectivity index |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rotenone | 0.01 | 0.48 | 51.7 |
| Deguelin | 0.02 | 19.40 | 1,148.5 |
| Tephrosin | 0.04 | 72.37 | 1,937.0 |

Indices above are transcribed from the paper; rounding the two displayed IC₅₀ values before division yields slightly different ratios. Rotenone and deguelin are known anchors. Tephrosin is a deliberately chosen retrospective stress case. Cell selectivity gives an experimental prioritization criterion and cannot establish clinical safety. [Assay table](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4RA08652J)

## Hypothesis

When two constituents co-occur in active mixtures, an assay on a sample that separates them can provide more decision value than an assay on the sample predicted to be most active. A discriminating sample must also be comparable in solvent, dose and measurement protocol. Otherwise a change in response could have an easier explanation. If every sample contains both constituents in the same proportions, nominate a separation experiment or abstain.

The follow-up found deguelin in all six newly profiled extracts except the *Pachyrhizus erosus* leaf extract. Rotenone was absent from the two *Cnestis palala* extracts. These observations illustrate separability. They belong to a later acquisition and must stay outside the historical model inputs. [Follow-up, Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4RA08652J)

## What would change our minds

- No identifiable contrast between candidate features after grouping ions and correcting for batch → the data support a family-level claim at most.
- The decision policy fails to outperform choosing the strongest predicted extract → report the baseline as a cheaper choice for this collection.
- A benefit disappears after withholding plant genera → botanical relatedness may account for the apparent effect.
- Follow-up identity matches are ambiguous → demonstrate extract-level decision making and remove compound-recovery claims.
- The rubric demands a named protein → reconsider the endpoint before proceeding. PTP1B has direct biochemical assays on isolated plant compounds and a separate kinetic dataset, but would require its own data audit. [Dodonaea study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fchem.2018.00040) · [Allophylus kinetics](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/63568/)
